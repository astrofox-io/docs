---
title: Reactors
description: Map audio, beats, or generated motion to Astrofox controls.
---

A reactor converts a source signal into a normalized output that can drive one
or more compatible controls. This is how you make scale pulse with bass,
rotation follow treble, or an effect appear on each beat.

## Create a reactor

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a reactor">
    In the Reactors panel, choose **Add reactor**. Select the new reactor to
    open its detailed controls and live meters above the player.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose an output mode">
    Pick an audio, beat, or static mode based on the motion you want.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tune the source">
    For spectrum modes, drag the selection over the frequency and level region
    you want to follow. Adjust Max dB, smoothing, hold, and decay while audio
    plays.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Bind a control">
    Select a display or effect. On a compatible numeric control, choose the
    reactor and set the value range the signal should traverse.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Output modes

| Family | Modes | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spectrum | Add, Subtract, Forward, Reverse, Cycle | Continuous motion driven by the selected audio band |
| Beat | Beat Trigger, Beat Envelope | Discrete hits or a pulse that decays after each detected beat |
| Static | Static Forward, Static Reverse, Static Cycle | Time-based motion that does not require audio |

**Add** and **Subtract** offset the control around its base value. **Forward**
and **Reverse** map the signal through the chosen range in opposite directions.
**Cycle** alternates direction. Static modes generate the same types of motion
at a configurable speed.

## Tune the response

- **Spectrum selection** isolates low, middle, or high frequencies and an
  amplitude range. Bass sits toward the low-frequency end; vocals and melody
  tend to occupy the middle; cymbals and air sit higher.
- **Max dB** changes the level treated as full output. Move it toward zero for
  loud sources and lower it for quiet sources.
- **Smoothing** steadies jittery motion at the cost of slower response.
- **Hold Time** keeps peaks active before they fall.
- **Decay** controls how quickly the value returns after a peak or beat.
- **Sensitivity** changes how readily beat modes trigger.
- **Speed** controls the rate of static modes.

## Practical patterns

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Bass pulse" icon="audio-waveform">
    Select the low end of the spectrum and bind Forward output to scale or Bloom strength.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Snare flash" icon="zap">
    Use Beat Trigger or Beat Envelope on opacity, brightness, or a stylized effect.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Slow rotation" icon="rotate-cw">
    Use Static Forward or Static Cycle on rotation for motion that continues during silence.
  </Card>
  <Card title="High-frequency shimmer" icon="sparkles">
    Select the upper spectrum and use a small range on RGB Shift, Film Grain, or displacement.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>
  One reactor can drive several controls. Use separate reactors when elements
  need different frequency bands, smoothing, beat sensitivity, or timing.
</Tip>
